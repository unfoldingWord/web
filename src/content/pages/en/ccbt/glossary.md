The terms in Church-Centric Bible Translation name the church, the translation, and the conditions that make church-led translation possible. Church-Centric Bible Translation (CCBT) is a paradigm focused on establishing a lingual church, with Bible translation as a means. The glossary distinguishes CCBT from Church-Based Bible Translation (CBBT) and Bible-Centric Bible Translation (BCBT), and it separates a trustworthy translation from a trusted one.

unfoldingWord uses these terms in the Church-Centric Bible Translation papers. CCBT is a subset of CBBT, and a trustworthy translation is distinct from a trusted one. For the paradigm itself, start at [What is Church-Centric Bible Translation?](/ccbt/).

<h2 id="church-centric-bible-translation">Church-Centric Bible Translation (CCBT)</h2>

Church-Centric Bible Translation (CCBT) is a [paradigm of Bible translation](/ccbt/) focused on the establishing and strengthening of a [lingual church](#lingual-church) using a translation process that is led by leaders of the church and done by believers translating into their own language, as an integral part of their [theological and spiritual formation](#theological-formation). The church-centric paradigm has as its focus the [establishing of the church](/ccbt/establishing-the-church/), with Bible translation as a means to that end.

<h2 id="church-based-bible-translation">Church-Based Bible Translation (CBBT)</h2>

Church-Based Bible Translation (CBBT) is a broader category: the church itself does the translation work. A Bible-centric project can still be church-based. [CCBT](#church-centric-bible-translation) is a subset of CBBT in which the aim is establishing the church, and translation is a means. Practitioners of the wider movement gather at [churchbased.bible](https://churchbased.bible/). These pages define the church-centric paradigm.

<h2 id="bible-centric-bible-translation">Bible-Centric Bible Translation (BCBT)</h2>

Bible-Centric Bible Translation (BCBT) is a paradigm of Bible translation focused on the production of a quality Bible translation using an academic and linguistic process that is usually led by a translation agency and functions independent of (and sometimes in advance of) the theological and spiritual formation of the [lingual church](#lingual-church). The Bible-centric paradigm has as its focus the production of a Bible translation, with life transformation as the intended outcome. See [Church-centric vs Bible-centric](/ccbt/church-centric-vs-bible-centric/).

## Church-centric

To be church-centric is to evaluate everything from the viewpoint of the needs of establishing and equipping the church (Acts 14:21–23; 15:40–41; Eph. 4:11–16). The ultimate objective of missions is the establishing of the church in each people group in sound doctrine, with mature leadership and the ability to understand the Word of God and defend sound doctrine for the glory of God. See [establishing the church](/ccbt/establishing-the-church/).

## Lingual church

A lingual church is a portion of the [global church](#global-church) that is linguistically homogeneous, speaking either the same language or variants that have a sufficient degree of mutual intelligibility, and including all traditions that are faithful expressions of sound doctrine (Eph. 4:1–6; 1 Tim. 6:3; Tit. 2:1). CCBT is concerned with establishing and strengthening that portion of the church, in the language it speaks.

## Established church

An established church is a church that is firmly rooted and grounded in sound doctrine (orthodoxy; cf. Eph. 4:11–16; Col. 2:5–7; 1 Cor. 15:58) and is characterized by faithful living (orthopraxy; cf. Eph. 4:1; Col. 1:10; 1 Thess. 2:12). Not synonymous with a “planted” church. Not the classic three-self rubric (self-supporting, self-propagating, self-governing), which addresses independence, not doctrinal integrity. See [establishing the church](/ccbt/establishing-the-church/).

## Global church

The global church is the household of God (Eph. 2:19), the body of Christ (Eph. 1:22–23), the sum total of all believers in the one faith (Eph. 3:8–10), from all languages and cultures (Matt. 28:19–20), including all traditions that are faithful expressions of sound doctrine (Eph. 4:4–5), at the current time. By extension, the universal church is the global church through all time.

## Local church

A local church is a portion of the [global church](#global-church) comprised of baptized believers in close geographic proximity that meet together regularly to worship God through Jesus Christ, to be exhorted from the Word of God, and to celebrate the Lord’s Supper under the guidance of duly appointed leaders. This term names the minimum qualifications that distinguish a local church in any people group from other gatherings of believers (e.g., a Bible study, a village meeting).

## Church network

A church network is interconnected [local churches](#local-church) that share a common leadership structure and doctrinal persuasion. This term includes formally-structured, centralized networks such as some denominations, as well as less-structured, decentralized networks such as some house church movements. Church networks translate open-licensed resources under the [Gateway Languages Strategy](/ccbt/gateway-languages/).

## Regional church

A regional church is [local churches](#local-church) that share a common geographical location (e.g., a city “the church in Ephesus” (Rev. 2:1) or a region “churches of Galatia” (Gal. 1:2)) and, frequently, cultural distinctives (e.g., “the Western church,” “the church of the Global South”). This term refers to the cross-section of all portions of the [global church](#global-church) in a given region, regardless of their affiliation or patterns of existing relationships and leadership structures.

## Gateway languages

Gateway languages (GLs) are the smallest number of Languages of Wider Communication that covers 100% of the languages spoken collectively by the people groups of the world through patterns of multilingualism. In practice the strategy names about 50 such languages. Majority status alone does not place a language in that set. See the [Gateway Languages Strategy](/ccbt/gateway-languages/).

## Other languages

In the Gateway Languages strategy, other languages (OLs) are all languages that are not considered [gateway languages](#gateway-languages). Not a synonym for “minority languages.” Some other languages are large and prestigious but may not be used by the [regional church](#regional-church) as a source language from which to translate. The church that is bilingual in a Gateway Language translates into its own other language.

## Gateway Languages Strategy

The Gateway Languages Strategy equips the [global church](#global-church) with the content, tools, and training needed for Bible translation and for creating theologically sound biblical resources, by providing them under open licenses and collaborating with [church networks](#church-network) to translate them into the ~50 Languages of Wider Communication that cover the global church through multilingualism. The church that is bilingual in a Gateway Language then translates into its own language. See [Gateway Languages](/ccbt/gateway-languages/).

## Trustworthy translation

A trustworthy translation is the objective reality of a translation that faithfully and clearly communicates the meaning of the original languages as it was communicated to the original hearers in their cultural, historical, and linguistic context. Trustworthiness names the text. Trust names the church’s reliance on it. See [trustworthy and trusted](/ccbt/trustworthy-and-trusted/).

## Trusted translation

A trusted translation is the subjective perception of the trustworthiness of a translation by the [lingual church](#lingual-church) that speaks that language, as indicated by their reliance on it for all matters pertaining to “faith and good doctrine” (1 Tim. 4:6). A translation can be trusted and not trustworthy, or trustworthy and unused. See [trustworthy and trusted](/ccbt/trustworthy-and-trusted/).

## Theological formation

Theological formation, as used here, is the biblical model of establishing the church in the gospel (kerygma) and teaching (didache), equipping them to build themselves up to maturity through obedience to Christ, and encouraging them to persevere through hardship to that end (cf. Acts 14:21–23; Eph. 4:11–16). In CCBT, believers translate into their own language as an integral part of that formation. See [establishing the church](/ccbt/establishing-the-church/).

## Equipping servant

An equipping servant is a proposed term for the New Testament diakonos function: translocal servants who move between churches to strengthen, encourage, and equip believers for the work of ministry, interdependent with local elders. Not the later “office of deacon” as a local board. People named diakonos in the New Testament include Epaphras, Tychicus, Timothy, Phoebe, Apollos, and Paul. See [equipping servants](/ccbt/equipping-servants/).

## Two obstacles

The two obstacles are language and legal. Language: the training, exegetical resources, and tools needed to translate with excellence are often available only in English. Legal: licenses on those resources often restrict who may use, translate, adapt, and redistribute them. In 2001 a Papua New Guinea church waited more than ten years for outside help, began translating on its own, and met both obstacles. See the [Gateway Languages Strategy](/ccbt/gateway-languages/) and [open-licensed biblical content](/ccbt/open-licensed-biblical-content/).

## Christian Commons

The Christian Commons is unrestricted biblical resources that can be legally translated, adapted, built on, revised, redistributed, and used, by anyone, without hindrance. Owners release this core of discipleship resources under open licenses, such as Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 (CC BY-SA 4.0), which permit translation, adaptation, distribution, and use without prior permission or royalties. See [open-licensed biblical content](/ccbt/open-licensed-biblical-content/).

## Whole Bible for the Whole Nation

Whole Bible for the Whole Nation is the aim that [church networks](#church-network) in a nation translate the whole Bible, not only a New Testament or portions, into the languages their churches need, as they establish the church among the least reached. It reintegrates church expansion, [theological formation](#theological-formation), and Bible translation, using open-licensed Gateway Language resources so the church can produce and keep the Scriptures itself.

## translationCore

translationCore is an open-source platform for checking and managing Bible translation projects. Translators use it to check a translation against multiple sources and the original languages, with just-in-time training that gives translators guidelines as they work. The church uses it as a tool and retains the responsibility to affirm a translation. See [trustworthy and trusted](/ccbt/trustworthy-and-trusted/).

## Open Bible Stories

unfoldingWord Open Bible Stories is 50 key stories of the Bible, from Creation to Revelation, for evangelism and discipleship, in text, audio, and video. It increases understanding of the historical and redemptive narrative of the entire Bible. Churches start with Open Bible Stories before they draft Scripture. See [how churches translate](/ccbt/how-churches-translate/).

## Parachurch organization

A parachurch organization is an institution that (usually) crosses [church network](#church-network) boundaries and serves alongside the church by specializing in tasks that [local churches](#local-church) may not be able to accomplish individually. Bible agencies function in this way. In a [church-centric](#church-centric) view, such organizations evaluate their work from the needs of establishing and equipping the church, rather than from the production of a specialized product.
