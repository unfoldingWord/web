  - type: prose
    body: |-
      That is the objective of missions in each people group. Established names sound doctrine and faithful living, planted names a new congregation, and three-self names independence from outsiders. Church-Centric Bible Translation (CCBT) treats translation as a means of establishing that church.

  - type: prose
    title: Definitions
    background: mist
    body: |-
      A few key definitions before we go any further, each of these from Tim Jore, *From Unreached to Established* (2018):

      **Established church:** a church that is firmly rooted and grounded in sound doctrine (orthodoxy; cf. Eph. 4:11–16; Col. 2:5–7; 1 Cor. 15:58) and is characterized by faithful living (orthopraxy; cf. Eph. 4:1; Col. 1:10; 1 Thess. 2:12). Not synonymous with a “planted” church. Not the classic three-self rubric (self-supporting, self-propagating, self-governing), which addresses independence, not doctrinal integrity. See also [established church](/ccbt/glossary/#established-church).

      **Church-Centric Bible Translation (CCBT):** a paradigm of Bible translation focused on the establishing and strengthening of a lingual church using a translation process that is led by leaders of the church and done by believers translating into their own language, as an integral part of their theological and spiritual formation. The church-centric paradigm has as its focus the establishing of the church, with Bible translation as a means to that end. See also [What is Church-Centric Bible Translation?](/ccbt/)

      **Bible-Centric Bible Translation (BCBT):** a paradigm of Bible translation focused on the production of a quality Bible translation using an academic and linguistic process that is usually led by a translation agency and functions independent of (and sometimes in advance of) the theological and spiritual formation of the lingual church. The Bible-centric paradigm has as its focus the production of a Bible translation, with life transformation as the intended outcome. See also [church-centric vs Bible-centric](/ccbt/church-centric-vs-bible-centric/).

  - type: prose
    title: Why it matters for the church
    body: |-
      To be [church-centric](/ccbt/glossary/#church-centric) is to evaluate everything from the viewpoint of the needs of establishing and equipping the church (Acts 14:21–23; 15:40–41; Eph. 4:11–16). The ultimate objective of missions is the establishing of the church in each people group in sound doctrine, with mature leadership and the ability to understand the Word of God and defend sound doctrine for the glory of God.

      Evangelism and planting begin a congregation, but establishing is the work of rooting that congregation in sound doctrine and faithful living. A planted congregation can still be carried about by every wind of doctrine, and a self-supporting, self-propagating and self-governing church can still teach error. The three-self rubric measures whether the church can stand on its own, while establishing measures whether the church is rooted in the faith and living it.

      CCBT treats translation as a means of forming a [lingual church](/ccbt/glossary/#lingual-church). Believers translate as they are established in the gospel and the teaching. The church builds capacity first, and from that capacity it produces a [trustworthy](/ccbt/trustworthy-and-trusted/) translation it can keep.

  - type: prose
    title: How it works
    body: |-
      Acts 14:21–23 is the pattern: after preaching the gospel in Derbe and making many disciples, Paul and Barnabas returned to the churches they had already begun:

      > …strengthening the souls of the disciples and encouraging them to continue in the faith. “We must endure many hardships to enter the kingdom of God,” they said. Paul and Barnabas appointed elders for them in each church, praying and fasting as they entrusted them to the Lord, in whom they had believed. (Acts 14:22–23, BSB)

  - type: steps
    intro: 'Their work had this order:'
    items:
      - title: Evangelize
        body: Preach the gospel and make disciples.
      - title: Strengthen and encourage
        body: Form the church in the faith, including the honesty that hardship is part of entering the kingdom.
      - title: Appoint elders
        body: Set local leaders in place, then commit the church to the Lord.

  - type: prose
    body: |-
      The New Testament keeps planting joined to strengthening and appointing elders, because a church that is not yet rooted will not endure. Christ gives apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors and teachers to equip the saints for the work of ministry, so the body builds itself up (Eph. 4:11–16). [Equipping servants](/ccbt/equipping-servants/) move among churches to do that work without replacing local elders.

  - type: cards
    title: Established, planted and three-self
    columns: 3
    background: mist
    items:
      - title: Established
        body: |-
          **What it measures:** Sound doctrine and faithful living; mature leaders who can understand and defend the Word

          **What it does not measure:** Buildings, budgets or independence from outsiders
      - title: Planted
        body: |-
          **What it measures:** A congregation has begun

          **What it does not measure:** Whether it is rooted, led and able to teach
      - title: Three-self
        body: |-
          **What it measures:** Self-supporting, self-propagating, self-governing

          **What it does not measure:** Doctrinal integrity; a church can be independent and still unfaithful

  - type: prose
    title: Movemental and institutional churches
    body: |-
      Movemental churches and institutional churches both belong to the one church, and churches change forms over time: they house gatherings in buildings and turn mentoring into schools. Those forms remain faithful when the church still establishes and equips, forming saints in sound doctrine and raising leaders who can handle the Word. An institutional church that recovers those functions can serve churches that are multiplying. A movemental church that evangelizes but never strengthens or appoints elders has planted without establishing.

  - type: prose
    title: Scripture
    body: |-
      Jesus sent the church to make disciples of all nations, baptizing them and teaching them to obey all he commanded (Matt. 28:16–20). Through the church the manifold wisdom of God is made known (Eph. 3:10). The working pattern is Acts 14:21–23, above. Christ gives gifts so the saints do the work of ministry and the body is no longer tossed by every wind of doctrine (Eph. 4:11–16). John’s vision is a people purchased from every tribe and language, made a kingdom and priests (Rev. 5:9–10). That end keeps missions aimed at the church.
